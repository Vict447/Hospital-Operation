# Hospital-Operation
📘 Project Overview
This project presents a comprehensive Healthcare Analysis Dashboard built to evaluate hospital performance across patient activity, financial outcomes, clinical workload, and monthly operational trends. The dashboard transforms raw data into actionable insights that support better decision‑making, operational efficiency, and patient‑centred care.

📊 Key Insights
1. Patient & Financial Performance
The dashboard reveals a slight 1% decline in patient volume, yet overall billing increased by 3%, totalling £208M. This suggests improved case complexity management or stronger billing accuracy.

Average billing per patient (£25,463) shows a modest 1% decrease, highlighting opportunities to refine documentation and coding practices.

The average length of stay (16 days) remains stable, indicating consistent care pathways. Even a one‑day reduction could significantly improve bed turnover and reduce pressure on clinical teams.

2. Billing Distribution Across Admission Types
Billing is almost evenly distributed across:

Urgent (35%)

Emergency (33%)

Elective (32%)

This balance reinforces the need for flexible staffing, rapid diagnostics, and strong urgent care pathways. Urgent cases carry the highest financial and clinical impact.

3. Clinical Workload Distribution
The top clinicians handled between 20 and 27 patients, showing a healthy workload balance. This supports consistent care quality, reduces burnout risk, and ensures equitable patient distribution.

4. Monthly Operational Trends
Monthly performance reveals clear seasonal patterns:

Peaks in January (732k) and August (730k)

A dip in February (588k)

Softening in October (652k) and November (633k)

Recovery in December (656k)

These fluctuations represent real operational pressures. Understanding these cycles allows leaders to plan proactively — adjusting staffing, inventory, and patient flow strategies ahead of demand.

🎯 Strategic Recommendations
1. Strengthen Urgent Care Capacity
Urgent cases drive the highest billing share. Enhancing triage speed, diagnostic turnaround, and urgent care staffing can improve outcomes for time‑sensitive patients.

2. Investigate Declining Patient Volume
A 1% drop may signal changes in referral patterns, competition, or operational bottlenecks. Understanding the root cause helps stabilise patient inflow and protect long‑term performance.

3. Improve Billing Accuracy & Documentation
A slight dip in average billing per patient shows that small gaps in documentation or coding can accumulate. Strengthening these processes ensures clinical work is fully captured and reimbursed.

4. Target LOS Reduction
Even a one‑day reduction from the current 16‑day average can free beds, reduce congestion, and improve patient flow. Better discharge planning and early intervention pathways can make this achievable.

5. Maintain Balanced Clinical Workloads
The current distribution (20–27 patients) is healthy. Continued monitoring ensures clinicians remain supported, engaged, and able to deliver consistent care.

6. Align Staffing With Seasonal Demand
Peaks in January and August — and dips in February and November — highlight the need for flexible staffing models. Planning ahead reduces pressure on teams and improves operational resilience.
